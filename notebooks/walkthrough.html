About 90% of appearances in 1996–2016 are flagged as starts, against about 47% from 2017, which is the plausible value (5 starters out of about 10–11 players used). A model trained on the older seasons would read every current player as a bench player, so the field is excluded and watched by a monitoring check.

2. What counts as success: two outcome constructs¶

  • Volume (A2 prototype): a season in the league's top 20% by total Game Score.
  • Rate (recommended): a rotation season (1,000+ minutes, scaled for short seasons) in the top third of rotation players by Game Score per 100 possessions.

Either way, a contributor has at least two such seasons in career years 4–8, and players who leave the league count as non-contributors.

4. Choosing the model on the validation classes (2011-14)¶

Four approaches, each with three calibration options, are scored on the 2011-14 classes. The winner has the lowest average Brier score across the three horizons. The 2015-18 test classes play no part in the choice.

The calibrated logistic regression wins on both Brier score and AUC. With era-relative, per-possession features doing the work, a transparent linear model matches the tree ensembles. That makes it the right champion for a client who must maintain it: it is simpler to explain, audit and retrain. The best tree model (random forest) is kept as a challenger to cross-check scores.

6. Business translation: shortlists, thresholds and net benefit¶

Because the probabilities are calibrated, the decision threshold comes straight from the cost of mistakes: threshold = 1 / (1 + cost ratio). If missing a contributor costs three times a wasted offer, the threshold is 0.25.

Ranking quality is similar across groups and every judged group is within the ±0.05 calibration tolerance. Summary¶

  • After three seasons the model ranks future contributors far better than draft order (AUC 0.96 against 0.83), and its probabilities are calibrated, so the business can set the threshold from the cost of mistakes.
  • It is a transparent, maintainable champion with a challenger cross-check, per-player uncertainty, drivers and comparables, and an explicit "insufficient evidence" state.
  • Monitoring found a real data defect (the started flag) and flags real drift (the three-point era, a falling contributor rate). Off-season retraining under Model Review Board approval keeps it current.
  • The next evidence must come from people: the scout blind review (E3) and the shadow pilot (E4) in docs/evaluation_protocol.md.
